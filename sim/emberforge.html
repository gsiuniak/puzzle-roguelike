The forging guide — how this effect is built

The whole look is one word drawn many times onto the same spot, in a deliberate order. Each pass adds one quality of forged metal. From back to front:

Fire glowThe text painted in the fire colour with a big shadowBlur, drawn 2–4 times so the halo builds up. Layered passes stay punchy; one giant blur just goes muddy.
Drop shadowA soft, offset-down shadow that grounds the text so it floats above a busy board instead of getting lost in it.
Forge depthThe text re-stamped in a dark colour, nudged one pixel at a time along a direction. The stack of copies reads as the chunky 3D side of each letter.
Edge outlineA thick dark stroke with rounded joins, wrapping the front face. Keeps letters legible over anything and defines the silhouette.
Metal faceA vertical gradient — bright sheen up top, a sudden dark reflection line near the middle, then brightening again toward the bottom. That hard dark band is the whole trick: it's what makes a flat fill read as polished, reflective metal.
Inner sheenA thin bright stroke over the front rim, like a catch of light on a beveled edge.

Dialing it in

The reflection line is everything. Keep Reflection height near the vertical middle (≈50–55%) and the width thin. Make the line genuinely dark — much darker than the body. If your metal looks flat and cheap, your reflection line is too pale or too wide.

Let the sheen do the brightening, not the body. Keep the body colour mid-bright. Push the Top sheen toward white and the Lower glow bright — that two-tone (cool-bright top, warm-bright bottom) sells the rounded surface while the reflection line still reads.

Depth direction = opposite the light. If the light feels top-left, point Forge depth → Direction down-right (≈45°). Straight down (90°) gives a flatter, "stacked UI badge" look that's very game-y. A short depth (5–9px) is plenty.

Outline thicker than feels natural. At big sizes a 7–11px dark edge looks right; thin outlines disappear over textured backgrounds. Joins are rounded so sharp corners don't spike out.

Fonts

Cinzel is the free stand-in for Trajan — the classic carved-Roman face you see on fantasy posters and exactly what's in your screenshot, so it's the default here. Cinzel Decorative adds flourishes for splashier words; Marcellus SC is a lighter, more refined cousin; Metamorphous and MedievalSharp lean more storybook/runic. All load live, so switching re-renders instantly.

Getting it into your game

Hit Copy settings to put every value on your clipboard as JSON — drop those numbers straight into your own canvas draw code and you'll reproduce the look pixel-for-pixel. Or Export PNG on a transparent background to overlay the rendered text directly over your board. Export at 2× and display at half size for crisp edges on high-DPI screens.
